Quantum Image Segmentation

The complete pipeline, explained from scratch — intuition & formulas side by side
📌 Read me first — the big picture. We take an image and split it into foreground (the object) and background. Instead of doing everything classically, we use quantum circuits for the heavy parts: encoding the image, finding edges, and choosing the best foreground/background labelling. The pipeline has four stages — NEQR → QHED → QUBO → QAOA — and ends with a clean binary mask.

1 · The Problem (from scratch)

Image segmentation means partitioning an image into meaningful regions. The simplest, most useful version is foreground / background separation: decide, for every pixel, "is this part of the object I care about, or not?" The output is a binary mask (1 = foreground, 0 = background).

Why quantum? Three reasons, each used by one stage of our pipeline:

  • Superposition — one quantum state can hold all pixels at once (used by NEQR).
  • Interference — a single Hadamard gate computes all neighbour differences in parallel (used by QHED).
  • Quantum optimization — QAOA searches the exponential space of fg/bg labellings (used in the final stage).

2 · Quantum in 60 Seconds

Intuition

  • A bit is 0 or 1. A qubit can be both at once — a blend.
  • Measuring a qubit gives 0 or 1 with probabilities set by its "blend".
  • n qubits represent 2ⁿ states simultaneously — exponential capacity.
  • Gates rotate/flip qubits. We use just a few: H, X, Z, RX, RZ, CNOT.

The pieces we use

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ ,  |α|² + |β|² = 1
  • H (Hadamard): makes a superposition |0⟩→(|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2
  • X: bit-flip. Z: phase-flip (eigenvalues ±1).
  • RX(θ), RZ(θ): tunable rotations — the QAOA "knobs".
  • CNOT: entangles two qubits.

4 · The Four Stages

Stage 1 — NEQR: Quantum Image Encoding neqr.py

Intuition

Before a quantum computer can process an image, the image must become a quantum state. NEQR stores every pixel's position and brightness as plain 0/1 patterns (basis states), all added together in superposition.

Because nothing is approximated, you can decode it back perfectly — like a lossless zip. That's why we say it "preserves spatial information".

Formula

|I⟩ = (1/2ⁿ) Σy,x |f(y,x)⟩ ⊗ |y⟩ ⊗ |x⟩ |y⟩,|x⟩ = position registers (n qubits each)
|f(y,x)⟩ = colour register (q qubits, the pixel value)
Total qubits = 2n + q ·   Gates = O(22n·q)

RESULT Lossless ⇒ PSNR = ∞, SSIM = 1.0, MSE = 0.

Stage 2 — QHED: Quantum Edge Detection qsobel.py

Intuition

Edges are where brightness changes sharply. QHED amplitude-encodes a row of pixels and applies one Hadamard gate to the last qubit. By quantum interference, this computes the difference of every neighbouring pixel pair at once.

Big difference = edge; small = flat region. Do it for rows and columns, combine, and you get a full edge map using only log₂N qubits per line.

Formula

H on LSB:  (a, b) → ( (a+b)/√2 , (a−b)/√2 ) the (a−b) outputs are the pixel gradients
E(y,x) = √( gh² + gv² ) gh = row gradient, gv = column gradient

RESULT ODS = 0.539 on BSDS500 — matches classical Sobel (0.54).

Stage 3 — QUBO: Turning Segmentation into an Energy superpixel.py

Intuition

We group pixels into ~12–16 superpixels (small patches) so the problem is small enough for a quantum computer. Each superpixel gets a binary label z=0 (bg) or z=1 (fg).

  • Unary term hᵢ: from colour — does this patch look like fg or bg? (a GMM trained on user "seed" pixels decides).
  • Pairwise term Jᵢⱼ: neighbours with similar colour should share a label — unless QHED found an edge between them (then allow a cut).
  • Seeds (known fg/bg patches) are fixed and folded in, shrinking the problem.

Formula

E(z) = Σi hi zi + Σ(i,j) Jij (zi − zj)²
hi = log P(ci|BG) − log P(ci|FG)
Jij = β · e−‖ci−cj‖²/σ² · (1 − qhedij) qhedij = quantum edge strength on the shared boundary ← Stage 2 feeds in here
QUBO → Ising:  hIi = −hi/2 ,  JIij = −Jij/2
Stage 4 — QAOA: Quantum Optimization of the Labels qaoa_seg.py

Intuition

Now find the labelling with the lowest energy. QAOA builds a quantum circuit whose measurement probabilities concentrate on good (low-energy) answers.

  • Start all qubits in superposition |+⟩ — every labelling equally likely.
  • A cost layer tilts phases toward low-energy labellings.
  • A mixer layer shuffles probability between candidates.
  • Classical optimizer (COBYLA) tunes the angles (γ, β).
  • Measure → most likely bitstring → expand to a pixel mask.

Formula

HC = Σ hIi Zi + Σ JIij ZiZj    HM = Σ Xi
|ψ(γ,β)⟩ = UM(β) · UC(γ) · |+⟩⊗n UC=e−iγHC, UM=e−iβHM
(γ*,β*) = argmin ⟨ψ| HC |ψ⟩

DECODE argmax(probs) → bitstring → fg/bg superpixel labels.

6 · Uses & Benefits

Per-stage

StageWhat it is used forBenefit
NEQR
encoding		 Loads the image into a quantum state that later stages operate on. Provably lossless (PSNR=∞) — no edge or texture is blurred by the encoding; gives an honest, exact quantum substrate.
QHED
edge detection		 Finds object boundaries; produces the edge feature used by the QUBO. Needs only log₂N qubits per line; one Hadamard does all neighbour gradients; matches classical Sobel quality.
QUBO
formulation		 Expresses "good segmentation" as a single energy to minimize. Reduces the problem to ~12–16 variables (superpixels) and injects quantum edge info into the pairwise term — a real quantum-to-quantum feature handoff.
QAOA
optimization		 Solves the labelling: which superpixels are foreground vs background. Explores the exponential label space in superposition; hardware-ready circuit (RX/RZ/CNOT only); tunable depth p for accuracy.

Overall pipeline

  • End-to-end quantum: representation → features → optimization all use quantum circuits, not just one isolated step.
  • Honest & benchmarked: every quantum stage is compared to a classical baseline (Sobel, GrabCut) with real metrics.
  • Modular: NEQR, QHED, QUBO and QAOA are independent — each can be swapped or improved on its own.
  • Hardware roadmap: works today on a simulator; the same circuits scale onto real quantum devices as qubit counts grow.
  • Interpretable: the QUBO energy and the QAOA bitstring are fully inspectable — you can see why a pixel was labelled fg/bg.

7 · Honest Limitations

  • Simulation scale: runs on the lightning.qubit simulator, tractable to ~25 qubits. Full-image, per-pixel quantum segmentation needs future hardware.
  • State-preparation bottleneck (Ruan 2021): loading the image into amplitudes is O(N) classically — the quantum speedup in QHED is real only with efficient quantum loading (qRAM).
  • Proof of concept: at 8–12 qubits, QAOA is coarser than full-resolution classical GrabCut. The contribution is a correct, benchmarked, end-to-end quantum pipeline — not yet state-of-the-art accuracy.
